# NGHE autonomous construction unstructured test text

NGHE site deployment began in 2030 in a mixed soil zone on the outskirts of a new town. Company A regarded this work as a case of service transition beyond simple equipment sales, and the integrated control center set the goal of finishing at least about 38,000 m3 of earthwork during the first 72 h. The actual construction target at the site was 100,000 m3 in scale, but this operation was strongly in the nature of a verification that processes about 38% of the entire construction within the first 3 days. Conditions were attached that cost must be lowered by 25% compared with the existing method, the schedule shortened by 30%, and personnel accidents and carbon emissions both kept at 0.

From the night before deployment, the site maintenance team first installed two battery swap stations, an energy storage system, and an auxiliary charging system. Each BSS_T was filled with 12 fully charged battery packs, and the control memo recorded "19 units of equipment covered plus 5 spares secured". In the same time frame, the site supervisor turned on the RTDD dedicated network together with the ICCC and confirmed that the end-to-end latency was within 20 ms. The encryption latency had to come in at 2 ms or less, and in the actual authentication process the HSM digital signature and TLS 1.3 connection were judged normal.

In the morning, DMSS drones swept the entire work area and collected 3D point cloud data. ASPCS corrected the GPS-RTK reference points, and the ICCC and AI_ICP reflected this data in the BIM and digital twin initial model. The site terrain was mixed with irregular ground with a maximum slope of around 30°, and AI_ICP marked sections of more than 25° as hazardous sections and then created bypass route candidates. Simulation results of the excavation, hauling, unloading, and compaction processes were posted on the control screen, and the remote supervisor approved starting in Level 3 supervised autonomy mode because this was the first operation.

The equipment was offloaded sequentially, but the initial authentication and calibration proceeded simultaneously. 5 U-EEX excavators, 10 U-EDT dump trucks, 2 U-EDB bulldozers, and 2 U-ERC rollers attached to the RTDD network and then each initialized its sensor package. LiDAR, Radar, and Camera were turned on together, and the equipment that finished GPS-RTK correction and terrain data download reported the "ready" status first. One unit took a little longer to calibrate, but within 1 h all equipment was displayed as ready. The site supervisor rechecked the Keep-out Zone and the sensitive facility protection zones.

The construction start began quietly. AI_ICP divided excavation points and quotas among the U-EEX units and sent the U-EDT units real-time optimal routes to the unloading area. The excavators had to maintain a GPS-RTK route-following precision within +-5 cm, and loaded while calculating even the entry angle of the dump trucks. The bulldozers arranged the soil near the unloading area, and the rollers waited at first and were deployed after compaction sections had been sufficiently formed. The ICCC dashboard displayed location, health status, work progress, and battery status together.

For about 1 h after the construction start, the remote supervisor watched collision avoidance between equipment, loading precision, and route optimization results. After 2 h had passed, once all indicators were confirmed to be within the normal range, the transition to Level 4 full autonomy mode was approved. From then on, AI_ICP adjusted most of the process planning, equipment allocation, route updates, and battery swap sequence autonomously. Remote operator Team A performed swarm control of 10 or more units of equipment but did not operate them directly, and checked only important alarms.

At the 6th hour of operation, excavation and hauling at a level of about 580 m3 per hour were maintained. The control memo noted cumulative soil of 3,480 m3, a saving trend against the expected energy consumption, and normal equipment operation. At night, whether the noise of the electric equipment was 65 dB or less was measured continuously, and because it did not exceed the legal standard, work was not stopped. At the 8 h shift change, remote operator Team A handed over to Team B, and the auxiliary cognition AI raised fatigue alarms for personnel who had been in continuous control for 4 h or more.

About 10 h after the construction start, the battery SoC of U-EEX Unit 1 reached 15%. The equipment entered Eco Mode and then autonomously returned to BSS_T, and the battery pack replacement was finished within 5 min. The entire swap cycle including movement, replacement, and return was about 15 min. AI_ICP adjusted the swap sequence so that two or more units did not leave work at the same time. Because the two BSS_T ran in parallel, the sequential swap of all 19 units was handled without work stop. The discharged packs secured turnover through the built-in chargers and SCS 800V fast charging.

At the 23 h point, an automatic performance report was generated. The cumulative earthwork volume was about 13,340 m3, and there were no personnel accidents. Through Full-Electric operation, direct on-site carbon emissions were recorded as 0, and the average operating rate was at the 96% level. At the 24 h point, the cumulative volume rose to about 13,920 m3, and the remote operators rotated from Team C back to Team A.

Around then, the PHM module of AI_ICP caught the hydraulic system anomaly sign of U-EDT Unit 7. It was not yet a failure, but the pressure pattern was departing from the normal range, and AI_ICP moved this dump truck to a non-core route while automatically increasing the quotas of the other dump trucks. The maintenance team replaced the hydraulic module, and the maintenance had to be finished within 1 h. The actual downtime was about 50 min. Even during the maintenance, the entire process did not stop.

Afterward, the OTA route optimization patch was applied to the onboard system. The update proceeded in a rolling manner and finished without operation stop, and as the route optimization effects between equipment accumulated after Level 4, the hourly output approached about 600 m3. The 2 U-ERC rollers started full-scale ground compaction near the unloading area, and compaction uniformity was monitored in real time. The report at the 40 h point recorded cumulative soil of about 28,000 m3, zero accidents maintained, an operating rate of 95.8%, and system availability of 99.1%.

On the morning of D+2, the weather observation system forecast dense fog. It was about 48 h after the construction start. AI_ICP turned on the severe weather pre-preparation protocol, and afterward visibility actually dropped to 200 m or less. When the sensor confidence score dropped, AI_ICP proposed lowering from Level 4 to Level 3. The remote supervisor approved this, and equipment speed was limited to 5 km/h or less. The safety distance was increased to 2 times that of normal times. Each unit of equipment raised the perception priority of radar and ultrasonic sensors instead of optical sensors.

In the fog, an unexpected cyber event occurred. The HSM of U-EEX Unit 3 detected the forged digital signature of a received command packet. It appeared that an unauthorized external IP tried to inject commands through the RTDD network, and the security system immediately blocked the external public network. The communication path was changed to the internal closed security network. Within 1 min, control authority integrity was confirmed, and Hard-wired Safe Stop was not executed. A security threat log was posted on the ICCC dashboard, and the remote supervisor issued a security scan command to all equipment. No anomaly was found in the other equipment.

When the fog cleared and visibility recovered to 1 km or more, optical sensor confidence also returned to the normal range. The remote supervisor approved the return to Level 4, and the speed limit was lifted. However, because of operating in Level 3 for about 3 h, the hourly output dropped to the 400 m3 level. The control memo read "Productivity degradation due to fog, safety conditions prioritized".

On the night of D+2, a heavy rain forecast came in. It was at the level of 30 mm per hour. Right after the ICCC received the forecast, AI_ICP proposed three measures: securing maximum construction volume before the heavy rain, completing battery swaps preemptively, and designating safe evacuation positions. The maintenance team and BSS_T checked the battery status of all equipment, and the equipment that needed it finished swaps in advance.

As the rain grew stronger, the LiDAR and camera lens of U-EEX Unit 2 were covered 50% or more by sticky soil. The equipment autonomously operated the air blow and wipers, but contamination removal was not sufficient. The onboard system switched to radar and ultrasonic sensor priority mode and lowered the speed to 5 km/h or less. When a maintenance team worker approached near the equipment and attempted manual cleaning, U-EEX Unit 2 detected the pedestrian, expanded the Keep-out Zone to 2 times that of normal times, and then came to a full stop. Only after cleaning completion and the worker's withdrawal were confirmed did that equipment resume work.

Afterward, when the heavy rain intensified, the remote supervisor issued a forced work stop command. All equipment switched to Level 1 emergency stop mode and moved to the safe evacuation positions designated in advance by AI_ICP. The equipment waited until the heavy rain ended. This state lasted several hours, and the control screen displayed "Work stop maintained, safe evacuation completed".

After the heavy rain end was confirmed, DMSS drones were urgently dispatched. Because terrain changes after the heavy rain were large, the existing routes could not be reused immediately. ASPCS re-corrected the GPS-RTK reference points, and two ground subsidence zones were newly identified. The ICCC and AI_ICP updated the BIM/digital twin model. AI_ICP recalculated the work routes based on the updated terrain data and reissued the routes to bypass the subsidence zones.

After the site supervisor completed the safety inspection, the remote supervisor approved construction resumption. However, because it was right after severe weather, it did not go straight to Level 4 but resumed in Level 3 supervised autonomy mode. The equipment returned from the safe evacuation positions to the work area, and the excavators and dump trucks restarted excavation and hauling. The bulldozers performed road surface arrangement after the heavy rain first. After about 30 min of normal operation was confirmed, the remote supervisor approved the transition to Level 4 again.

After the heavy rain, there were many sections where the moisture content had changed, so the rollers' compaction pressure and number of passes were automatically adjusted. Night low-noise operation continued, and the maximum noise was kept at 63 dB. At the final 72 h mission completion point, the ICCC and AI_ICP automatically generated the comprehensive report. The actual construction time was about 64 h 40 min, excluding about 7 h 20 min of severe weather stops. Reflecting the base output of 580 m3/h and the Level 4 efficiency improvement, the cumulative earthwork volume became about 38,200 m3. The target of 38,000 m3 was met.

The final report recorded zero personnel accidents, net-zero carbon emissions, an average equipment operating rate of 95.2%, system availability of 99.1%, BIM-based construction error within +-3 cm, and GPS-RTK route-following precision of +-4.2 cm. The remote supervisor recorded "Although there were stops, the goal was achieved while keeping the safety conditions", and the ICCC classified this operation as a reference case of commercial-scale autonomous construction.
