# MUM-T attack maneuver unstructured test text

Ahead of an attack maneuver in inland hilly terrain, the tank company activated a MUM-T system combining three reconnaissance drones and a lightweight TDSS. In the operation area, ridges and valleys repeated, so there were many places where the tanks' line of sight was limited to 1-2 km, and because of villages and wooded areas there was a high possibility that enemy observation posts and anti-tank positions were concealed. The company commander had to fuse drone ISR and tank sensor cues in the TDSS to create integrated situational awareness (SA), and on that basis make decisions on changing the maneuver axis and responding to threats.

Before the operation started, the TDSS self-diagnosis was first performed in the company commander's tank. The TDSS confirmed that communication with the battalion server was normal, and checked the SA sharing, tactical analysis result reception, and threat alert display functions in normal communication mode. Drone 1 was deployed to take on surveillance 2-3 km ahead on the maneuver axis, Drone 2 the left flank, and Drone 3 the right flank. After the three drones launched sequentially, the video stream and detection metadata began to come into the TDSS, and the integrated SA screen was displayed on the HMI.

The company commander shared the drone surveillance status and expected enemy positions with the tank commanders of each platoon. The 1st Platoon took the lead, the 2nd Platoon the middle echelon, and the 3rd Platoon the rear guard and flank security. Before crossing the line of departure, the TDSS made the initial transmission of company SA summary information to the battalion server and began receiving the battalion's high-level tactical analysis results. At this point the communication state was normal, and drone video and tank sensor data both came in without delay.

When the attack maneuver started, the 1st Platoon moved at the head of the wedge formation. Drone 1 surveilled beyond the ridge about 2 km ahead of the lead, and the other two drones swept the left and right flanks widely. The TDSS continuously updated the threat priorities while comparing drone video, tank thermal sensors, and battalion analysis information together. The blind spots on both sides of the maneuver axis and the drone surveillance overlap zones were displayed on the company commander's HMI.

During the maneuver, Drone 1 picked up a heat source suspected to be an enemy observation post behind the forward ridge. The TDSS immediately raised an alert and, combining drone video and tank sensor cues, judged it as "high possibility of enemy observation post". The tactical analysis received from the battalion server stated that there was a high probability that enemy anti-tank positions were deployed in the area of that ridge. Based on this information, the company commander decided on the right bypass maneuver and linked the target information to the battalion through the TDSS.

When the bypass maneuver started, Drone 3 was redeployed ahead of the bypass axis. Drone 1 continued to check whether the enemy was leaving in the direction of the original observation post, and the TDSS updated SA even during the bypass. The company commander reported progress to the battalion while passing the control point. In this segment, communication quality was stable, and both the battalion analysis results and drone video were maintained normally.

Shortly afterward, Drone 1 Edge AI detected multiple small aerial vehicles in a wooded area 1.5 km ahead. The analysis result was a possibility of 3-4 FPV kamikaze drones. The TDSS displayed the threat direction, an approach speed of about 35 m/s, and an estimated time of arrival of about 43 s on the company commander's HMI. The company commander ordered all platoons: "Enemy FPV drone approach, dispersed maneuver and air-defense watch", and the subordinate platoons immediately spread out. The TDSS also urgently shared the enemy drone threat information with the battalion server.

After the FPV approach, the enemy simultaneously began electronic warfare jamming. On the TDSS communication state monitoring screen, the packet loss rate surged, and when the loss rate persisted above the threshold, the TDSS began the transition from normal communication to limited communication mode. Frame drops and delays occurred in the drone video stream. The TDSS commanded the three drones to stop video transmission and switch to Edge AI analysis mode. Instead of the video stream, the drones began sending only summarized track data to the TDSS.

Even in limited communication mode, survivability-focused functions were maintained. The TDSS updated minimum SA based on the summary track data, and the communication state "Limited" and the drone Edge mode switch completion status were displayed together on the HMI. The company commander confirmed that although video quality had been lowered, immediate alerts and threat priority refinement continued to operate. This transition was a graceful degradation performed on the basis of communication quality degradation.

During limited mode, one enemy FPV drone approached to 500 m in front of 1st Platoon Tank 3. Drone 1's Edge AI continued to maintain the track data, and the TDSS provided an immediate alert to the 1st Platoon. 1st Platoon Tank 3 received the alert and performed an evasive maneuver to avoid the FPV drone. Soon after, two enemy FPV drones approached toward the 2nd Platoon. The TDSS again provided a track-based alert, and the 2nd Platoon carried out air-defense watch and dispersed maneuver. One of these FPVs exploded nearby and caused minor damage to the external antenna of 2nd Platoon Tank 2, but combat capability was maintained.

The intensity of the enemy electronic warfare attack kept rising. The drone-tank data link became unstable, and after confirming that the remaining FPV threat had disappeared, the company commander ordered company assembly and damage check. Because communication was still in the limited state, the battalion report was attempted in abbreviated form. The TDSS maintained minimum SA relying not on video-based SA but on track data and local sensor cues.

As the jamming output grew stronger, GPS reliability also dropped sharply. The TDSS detected heartbeat loss with the drones and higher echelon. When the loss persisted for a certain time or more, the TDSS declared the transition from limited mode to communication blackout mode. External information exchange was logically blocked, and the local sensor-based survival loop was activated. The communication state "Blackout", standalone mode, and the elapsed time since the last received information were displayed on the HMI.

The three reconnaissance drones also detected on their own the heartbeat dropout with the company commander's tank. The drones switched to emergency broadcast mode and, without going through the company commander's tank, began transmitting the coordinates and type of threat detection data directly to nearby friendly tanks. The subordinate platoons combined the drone broadcasts and their own sensor cues to perform individual survival actions. The company commander judged that integrated command was difficult and allowed independent action to continue according to the existing operation order.

In the blackout state, two additional enemy FPV drones approached toward the 3rd Platoon. Drone 2 Edge AI tracked them and transmitted an alert by broadcast. The 3rd Platoon tanks fused the information received from the drone broadcast with their own sensor cues and made evasive maneuvers. One FPV crashed and exploded against cover near 3rd Platoon Tank 1, but no friendly damage occurred. Then Drone 3 Edge AI detected an enemy ATGM launch and broadcast the alert "ATGM launch, direction southwest". The 2nd Platoon received this alert, deployed a smoke screen, and then performed an evasive maneuver.

The company commander's tank maintained minimum situational awareness with only the last received SA information and its own sensor cues. The information reliability indication on the HMI decreased as time passed, and with battery depletion imminent, Drone 1 landed at its final position by its own judgment. The surveillance area shrank. The company commander compiled the damage status from a covered position. All tanks were combat-capable, Drone 1 was lost or deactivated, and Drone 2 and Drone 3 were operating on remaining battery.

As the enemy electronic warfare attack weakened, intermittent communication signals were picked up again. The TDSS first detected link recovery with Drone 2, but did not immediately return to normal mode and waited to confirm stability. When the link was maintained for a certain time or more, the TDSS began the transition from blackout mode to limited mode. Then it received Drone 2's track data accumulated during the blackout period and began SA resynchronization. Drone 3's link was also recovered in sequence, and surveillance area reallocation was attempted.

Voice communication between the company commander and the subordinate platoons was also recovered. Checking each platoon's damage status showed that all tanks were combat-capable. The connection between the TDSS and the battalion server was recovered later, and bandwidth recovered gradually. After confirming that connection stability was maintained, the TDSS began the transition from limited mode to normal communication mode. SA sharing, target information linking, and battalion analysis result reception resumed in sequence.

The company commander sent the comprehensive report on the blackout period to the battalion. The report included the enemy FPV and ATGM threat encounters, the progress of the electronic warfare attack, the minor antenna damage, the loss of Drone 1 due to battery depletion, and the operable state of the 2 remaining drones. The tactical analysis result of the battalion server was a judgment that the enemy electronic warfare unit had withdrawn and the remaining threat was small-scale.

After confirming stabilization of normal communication mode, the TDSS fully reactivated the operational activity groups. However, due to the loss of Drone 1, the available drones were reduced to 2, and the surveillance area sharing capability was also reduced. The company commander decided to perform reduced surveillance toward the objective with only Drone 2 and Drone 3. Then the company commander ordered maneuver resumption, and the subordinate platoons began attacking toward the objective again.

On the outskirts of the objective, Drone 2 confirmed a small-scale remaining enemy force. The TDSS updated the alerts and SA, and the battalion analysis result also supported that the remaining threat was limited. Reflecting this, the company commander decided to carry out the attack. The subordinate platoons suppressed the remaining enemy positions with tank fire, and after securing the objective began organizing defensive positions by platoon.

In the final report, the company commander notified the battalion that the objective was secured. Drone 3 landed due to final battery exhaustion, and Drone 2 continued the final surveillance flight on remaining battery. During the operation, the key transitions proceeded from normal communication to limited communication, from limited communication to blackout, from blackout to limited, and from limited to normal. Each transition was performed on the basis of observations and conditions such as packet loss, heartbeat loss, link stability recovery, and bandwidth recovery. Even in a situation where video was cut off and the higher echelon connection was lost, the TDSS maintained survivability-focused functions through Edge AI, emergency broadcast, and local sensor fusion.
